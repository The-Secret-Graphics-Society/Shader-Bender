About

Step into the immersive world of Shader Bender, where your hands and arms become the ultimate tools of elemental mastery. In this sandbox experience, you will harness the raw power of the elements by controlling lightning, fire, water, and air through dynamic gestures and movements. Experiment, create, and bend the elements to your will. Whether you're crafting dazzling displays of elemental synergy or testing your skills on the dojo's poor potted plants, Shader Bender offers a number of possibilities for creativity and discovery.

How to Play

Setup

To start your journey in Shader Bender, simply download and run the executable file. Once the game launches, press C to calibrate your position. Ensure your entire body is visible to your computer's webcam, as there's a 5-second timer for calibration. To ensure a consistent experience, get into position by standing straight with your arms outstretched in a T-pose.

Bending Elements

Shader Bender allows you to wield the powers of fire, water, wind, and lightning. Each element has unique controls and mechanics, so mastering them all will unlock your full potential.

Water - Activate by raising your left hand above your head, then lower it.

Fire - Activate by raising your right hand above your head, then lower it. Call down meteors by raising and lowering your right hand, and extend your arms and place your hands together to shoot flames from your hands.

Wind - Activate by raising and holding both of your hands above your head.

Lightning - Activate by when water or fire aren't active by extending your arms forward and placing your hands together.

The Team

Filip Karlsson
Lightning Effects Gesture Recognition Game Design

My primary focus on this project was developing the calibration system and adding to the gesture recognition system. The most visually striking feature I contributed was the lightning effect, inspired by a research paper on generating lightning-style textures using a tessellation shader on the GPU. By incorporating a Bezier curve function into the effect, the lightning effect was designed to arc and split dynamically with the environment, creating realistic and strikingly fast visuals. Through this project, I gained valuable experience in motion tracking, gesture recognition, and tessellation/geometry shaders, significantly improving my technical skills in these areas.

Martin Håkansson
3D Modeling Art Environmental Effects

My focus on this project was to model and texture the 3D models in the scene. All models such as the player character, the dojo, plants etc are made from scratch, so no external assets are used in the game. All models are made in blender, the textures are made in the blender shader node editor. Some of the assets are made using geometry nodes inside blender. The character is also rigged, and has is posed with inverse kinematics tracked from the user movement. As for environmental effects, I created the wind effects on the surrounding - which changes in intensity depending on the state of the wind dome. The wind is created with C# code, and has attributes passed into a vertex shader.

Lucas Åkesson
Environmental Effects Post Processing Audio

My contribution to Shader Bender involved working on the environmental setting of the experience. This involved creating weather effects such as rain, lightning and the clouds in the sky, getting the right look using post-processing, and enhancing the experience with an ambient soundscape. All weather effects were created using Unity's particle systems, and manipulating these to achieve the desired effect. The clouds are made using a Unity URP shader to achieve a fake volumetric-rendering effect. As such, the clouds are just a shader on a simple plane, with some added effects to make them feel more 3D. The post-processing was done using global volume, with effects such as bloom, vignette, and white balance. All of the sounds I included in the soundscape, such as rain and lightning, and the background track of the experience, were created using audio from FreeSound.

David Giraldo
Wind Effects Volume Rendering HLSL

In the Shader Bender project, I contributed by researching and implementing the wind-bending power using volume rendering techniques. I used HLSL to design a dynamic, visually compelling procedural effect for the wind dome and optimized its interaction with the player environment. I ensured seamless communication between the CPU and GPU for real-time updates. This work deepened my expertise in shader programming, procedural noise generation, and volume rendering techniques.

William Rolf
Fire Effects Fire Interaction Audio

For my contribution to the Shader Bender project, I focused primarily on the fire element and object destruction part of the interaction. My research led me to finding resources for both volumetric and particle-based fire systems, the latter of which was demonstrated in a Q&A session with developers of the game “Diablo III” and showed that layered particle systems yield far better performance. Therefore I set about creating shaders that utilize layered particle systems for all kinds of fire interactions that I set up, from calling down meteors to casting a flamethrower and burning the environment around the player in real-time using motion tracking data. This project taught me how to handle complex interactions combined with particle system VFX techniques.

udon
Water Effects Motion Capture Shaders Web Development

My contribution to the project was primarily with the motion capture and the water element. I led the effort to get MediaPipe working in Unity, and focused most of my initial efforts on getting the motion capture working properly so that other systems could be worked on in parallel. Once this was done, my advanced graphics contribution was implementing the water element, which was done in HLSL. The effect itself was handled through a particle system - which controlled emission of spheres with this shader, a render texture that I used as a "wetness map" to draw puddles of water on the ground, and projective volumes that emulated caustics. In addition, I moved the wind blowing effect from the CPU onto the GPU, handling vertex displacement in a shader. This project has deepened my knowledge of shader graphics and motion capture.
