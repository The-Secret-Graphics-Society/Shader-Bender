Environmental Effects and Soundscape

The rain and lightning effects were all created using Unity's particle systems. For the rain, it spawns a high rate of particles that simulate rain drops, and despite not being visible in the project, also spawns water rings on the surface upon collision with the ground. The lightning in the background is also a particle system that utilizes noise to create a random variation to a straight line that simulates lightning. Upon birth, the lightning lets off a bright light at the top, and upon impact it also generates a bright light as well as sparks flying off the ground. The clouds of ShaderBender were made to look like volume rendered clouds, despite only being a shader on a simple plane. It was made this way to limit the computational power required, as the clouds are not a main feature of the game. It works by passing over a simple noise map, with added effects and some subdivisions to the plane to make it look a bit more 3D. The mountain in the background was made using the Blender plugin A.N.T Landscape, and was created to somewhat resemble Mt. Fuji. For the post-processing, effects such as bloom, vignette and white balance were utilized to create a night time environment. Lastly, the soundscape of the experience was created utilizing sounds from FreeSound.org, that were then mixed together using the software Audacity.

Goals and Motivation

Our goals when first drafting up what we wanted out of the project were as follows:

  • Create an engaging, open-ended interactive experience combining advanced visual and interaction techniques.
  • Utilise webcam-based motion capture as the primary input method, ensuring it is effective and engaging.
  • Enhance team expertise in shader programming, motion capture, and real-time rendering methods.

Our motivation for this project grew from the exciting opportunities it presented for exploration and learning. By creating an open-ended interactive experience without a traditional game loop, we aimed to develop something akin to an exhibit at an interactive museum - a space for creativity and discovery. The novelty of integrating motion capture, a relatively new area for our team, added an intriguing challenge that pushed us to expand our technical and design capabilities. In addition, the project offered a rich learning environment, encompassing diverse areas such as volume rendering, game character animation, motion capture techniques, ray marching, and shader programming.

Obstacles and Challenges

The project faced several technical and design challenges. The main hurdle early on was getting MediaPipe working within Unity, which complicated the integration of webcam-based gestures. The open-ended nature of the experience risked alienating users due to unclear interaction, which was a problem that further complicated the lack of recognisable gestures that we could track. Volume rendering in Unity was computationally intensive, requiring optimizations to balance performance and visual quality. Vertex displacement was a problem encountered during the last stretch of development, and time had to be spent optimising the effects to run on the GPU rather than CPU. Finally, achieving realistic environmental effects like smoke, weather, and day-night cycles posed challenges in maintaining aesthetic fidelity without exceeding computational limits.
